Textbook Lab Themes

We propose two labs for Advanced Machine Learning, on optimization and training (Lab 1) and on regularization and generalization (Lab 2). The topics come from Goodfellow et al., chapters 6 to 8. Each theme was prototyped as a class experiment, with one detail page per theme.

Optimizer race

Idea
Gradient descent, momentum, RMSProp and Adam each work best in their own range of learning rates (Goodfellow 8.3, 8.5). On a narrow valley, plain gradient descent zigzags, and it diverges when the learning rate is too large.
Students do
Each student runs a few training cells on a small classifier with different optimizers and learning rates, after a 2-D warm-up. The class builds one plot of final loss against learning rate, one curve per optimizer.
Competition idea
Lowest training loss within a fixed number of optimizer steps.

Key paper: Kingma & Ba 2015, Adam

Main experiment · 45 min

Going deep

Idea
In deep plain networks the gradient shrinks or grows from layer to layer, so training fails. Good initialization, BatchNorm and residual connections help in different ways (Goodfellow 6.3, 8.4, 8.7.1).
Students do
Each student trains networks of several depths and variants (sigmoid, ReLU, BatchNorm, residual). The class builds one plot of test accuracy against depth, one line per variant.
Competition idea
Highest test accuracy for a deep network with a fixed training budget.

Key papers: He et al. 2016, Deep Residual Learning; Ioffe & Szegedy 2015, Batch Normalization

Class ensemble

Idea
Averaging many networks cancels the part of their errors that differs between them, while the shared part stays (Goodfellow 7.11, 7.12).
Students do
Each student trains a small network on an own subset of the data and submits its predictions. The class plots ensemble accuracy against the number of models averaged.
Competition idea
Best ensemble built from a fixed number of models, scored on hidden data.

Key paper: Lakshminarayanan et al. 2017, Deep Ensembles

Main experiment · 50 min

Adversarial examples

Idea
A tiny change to each pixel, computed from the network's gradient, makes an accurate MNIST model give wrong answers. Training on attacked images keeps accuracy under attack; L2 and dropout do not help much (Goodfellow 7.13).
Students do
Each student trains a model with one training variant and attacks it with FGSM and PGD. The class builds one plot of accuracy against attack strength, one curve per variant.
Competition idea
Highest accuracy under a server-side PGD attack, with a minimum clean accuracy.

Key paper: Goodfellow et al. 2015, Explaining and harnessing adversarial examples

Alternative and advanced script

The advanced topics give a second, more research-oriented option for each lab: one main experiment is swapped for an advanced one (highlighted below). Details are on the AML Advanced Topics page.

Lab 1

  1. Framing + 3D demo15 min
  2. Optimizer race25 min
  3. Edge of stability or Loss landscape (advanced)45 min
  4. Speedrun competition35 min
  5. Debrief15 min

Edge of stability replaces Going deep as the main class experiment: together with the Optimizer race warm-up (2-D or 3D), both show the 2/lr rule. Loss landscape is a second option: it pairs naturally with Going deep, since it shows why residual connections make deep nets trainable.
